PREHISTORIC BEHAVIOURAL RESEARCH ARCHIVE · CASE 66-C

Did Dinosaurs
Smoke Weed?

An unconventional investigation into plant evolution, dinosaur behaviour, and one remarkably suspicious fossil record.

A Satirical Scientific Investigation
01 / The Question

The hypothesis nobody asked for.

For decades, palaeontologists have reconstructed dinosaur behaviour from bones, footprints, teeth and fossilised plant material. Yet one apparently obvious question has received considerably less attention:

If dinosaurs were surrounded by plants for millions of years, how confident are we that they were interested only in eating them?

This investigation examines a deliberately unconventional hypothesis: that certain dinosaurs may have encountered Cannabis-like plants or chemically similar vegetation during the Cretaceous period.

There is currently no credible evidence that dinosaurs consumed cannabis, became intoxicated, or deliberately smoked plants. The case presented here is therefore a thought experiment disguised as an academic investigation.

02 / The Circumstantial Evidence

The evidence is suspiciously interesting.

01

Flowering Plants Existed

Flowering plants appeared during the Cretaceous and were already forming established ecosystems before the dinosaurs disappeared.

02

Dinosaurs Ate Plants

Fossil evidence shows that numerous herbivorous dinosaurs consumed plant material. The menu was considerably more complicated than the stereotypical image of dinosaurs eating nothing but leaves.

03

They Had Time

Dinosaurs dominated terrestrial ecosystems for roughly 165 million years. From a purely hypothetical perspective, that is an extraordinarily long period in which to encounter unusual vegetation.

03 / Chronology

A suspicious timeline.

≈ 135 million years ago

Flowering plants appear

Angiosperms emerge and begin expanding into ecosystems that dinosaurs already inhabit.

≈ 100–70 million years ago

Plant diversity increases

Flowering plants become increasingly important components of Cretaceous ecosystems.

≈ 74.6 million years ago

The Botanical Pompeii

Fossilised plant material discovered in New Mexico indicates that flowering plants were already thriving in mature forests millions of years before the final dinosaur extinction.

66 million years ago

The asteroid arrives

A catastrophic impact occurs. Non-avian dinosaurs disappear. The investigation abruptly loses its primary witnesses.

04 / Statistical Irresponsibility

The numbers tell a story.

165M Years of dinosaur dominance
66M Years since the extinction event
0 Confirmed dinosaur smoking witnesses

The third figure is, admittedly, somewhat problematic for the theory.

05 / Alternative Interpretations

There are problems with the theory.

A serious investigation must acknowledge inconvenient evidence.

No Smoking Devices

No prehistoric rolling papers, pipes, lighters, or suspiciously dinosaur-shaped ashtrays have been recovered.

No Chemical Proof

There is no established fossil evidence demonstrating that dinosaurs consumed cannabis or experienced its effects.

Plant ≠ Cannabis

The existence of flowering plants during the Cretaceous does not establish that cannabis itself was present in the form familiar today.

FINAL ASSESSMENT

Case remains open.

From a scientific standpoint, there is currently no evidence that dinosaurs smoked cannabis. From a completely unserious investigative standpoint, however, the witnesses have been dead for 66 million years and therefore remain remarkably unavailable for questioning.

The hypothesis is best regarded as satire inspired by genuine palaeontological facts—not as an established scientific theory.
